Investors are reassessing how they use gold as inflation signals become less straightforward, with market participants weighing not only headline consumer-price data but also interest-rate expectations and macro volatility.

Reuters reports that portfolio positioning is increasingly focused on the interaction between inflation persistence and central-bank policy paths, rather than a single monthly CPI print. That shift has kept attention on real yields, dollar direction, and geopolitical risk as co-drivers of precious-metals demand.

For asset allocators, the immediate implication is tactical: gold is being framed less as a one-variable inflation trade and more as a broader hedge in an environment where growth, rates, and price pressures can diverge across regions.

### Why it matters

If inflation slows unevenly while policy easing remains uncertain, demand for defensive allocations could stay elevated. In that setup, gold's role in portfolios may depend as much on rate volatility and risk sentiment as on inflation alone.
