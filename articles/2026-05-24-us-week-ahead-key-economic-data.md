U.S. markets head into a data-heavy week with investors focused on whether incoming numbers confirm a gradual cooling path for inflation and activity — or force another reset in interest-rate expectations.

Economists and market participants are watching a cluster of releases often used as policy signals, including growth updates, inflation-sensitive indicators and demand readings. Together, those reports can influence how quickly traders price in potential Federal Reserve rate cuts.

The setup matters because recent rate-cut bets have been sensitive to even modest surprises in inflation and labor-related data. A stronger-than-expected run could push expectations for easing further out, while softer prints may revive calls for earlier moves.

For businesses and households, the implications are practical: borrowing costs, mortgage pricing, credit conditions and risk appetite across equities and corporate debt can all move quickly when the policy outlook shifts.

### Why this is the main business-economy story this week

This topic fits the business-economy desk directly because it centers on macroeconomic releases and their impact on rates, markets and financing conditions — the core transmission channels for companies, consumers and investors.
