## What’s New

- Ask.com (formerly Ask Jeeves) has shut down its long-running consumer search product.
- The closure marks the end of one of the web’s early search brands, active in various forms since the late 1990s.
- The move underscores continued consolidation in search, where AI-assisted products and large-scale index operators dominate user traffic.


Ask.com, the search brand that began as Ask Jeeves, has shut down its consumer search service after nearly 30 years online. The closure ends one of the best-known early-era search destinations, which once differentiated itself with a question-and-answer style interface.

Over time, the search market consolidated around a handful of companies with larger indexing infrastructure, distribution deals, and advertising networks. In recent years, the category has shifted again toward AI-assisted search and answer engines, raising the technical and financial bar for smaller independent brands.

While Ask.com’s market share had long declined from its peak era, its shutdown is still notable as a marker of how quickly internet product cycles have accelerated—from portal-era web navigation to mobile-first discovery and now AI-mediated retrieval.

For users, the immediate impact is limited given existing alternatives. For the industry, the story is another signal that legacy web products face mounting pressure to either reinvent around new interaction models or exit saturated categories.
