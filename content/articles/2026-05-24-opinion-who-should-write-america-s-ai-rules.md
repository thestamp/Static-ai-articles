What happened
- Recent U.S. commentary has converged on one fault line in AI governance: whether federal officials should preempt state-by-state rules or set only a floor that states can build on.
- A reported White House discussion about vetting frontier models before release has added urgency to that debate by raising questions about safety, competition, and executive authority.

Why it matters
- A single national framework could reduce compliance fragmentation for model developers operating across all 50 states.
- A stronger state role could preserve faster policy experimentation on privacy, consumer protection, and public-sector AI procurement.

Our take
- The policy debate should not be framed as federal control versus state innovation. A workable path is a federal baseline for transparency and safety testing, with room for states to add targeted safeguards where local risk is higher.
- Any pre-release review regime should be narrowly scoped to high-capability systems and paired with clear due-process timelines; otherwise, it risks becoming either symbolic or anti-competitive.

What to watch
- Whether federal policymakers publish concrete model-risk thresholds rather than broad principles.
- Whether states pause, align, or accelerate their own AI rulemaking while federal standards are debated.
