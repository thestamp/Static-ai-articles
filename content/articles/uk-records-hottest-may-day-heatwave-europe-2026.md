The UK recorded its hottest day ever observed in May, reaching **34.8°C**, as unusually intense heat spread across parts of Europe during the holiday weekend.

According to *The Guardian’s* reporting, forecasters confirmed the new national May high and warned that prolonged warm spells are becoming more likely in a warming climate. The heat surge has raised concerns about public-health risks, infrastructure strain, and wildfire susceptibility in dry regions.

Climate scientists have repeatedly cautioned that human-caused warming is increasing the frequency and intensity of extreme heat events. This latest UK record adds to a broader pattern of temperature extremes being logged across the continent.

Authorities in multiple countries have urged residents to stay hydrated, avoid peak-afternoon exposure, and monitor vulnerable populations as temperatures remain elevated.

### Why this matters

Heat records that once occurred rarely are being broken more often, compressing adaptation timelines for cities, health systems, and energy grids. Policymakers face increasing pressure to strengthen heat preparedness while accelerating emissions cuts.
