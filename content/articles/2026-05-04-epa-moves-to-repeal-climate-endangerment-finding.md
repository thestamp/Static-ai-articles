U.S. regulators are preparing a major rollback in climate policy after the Environmental Protection Agency signaled plans to repeal the greenhouse-gas endangerment finding that has underpinned federal emissions rules for more than a decade.

What happened: The move would target the legal determination that greenhouse gases endanger public health and welfare, a core predicate used for vehicle and power-sector carbon rules.

Why it matters: Repealing the finding could reshape how — and whether — the federal government regulates carbon pollution, while setting up a high-stakes legal battle likely to influence long-term U.S. climate policy and business planning.

What to watch next:
- Formal EPA rulemaking text and timeline.
- Expected litigation from states, environmental groups, and industry stakeholders.
- Near-term effects on sector-specific emissions standards.
